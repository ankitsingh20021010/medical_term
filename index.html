This is not just a medical timeline.

This is the story of what happened to me from the day I first lost consciousness to the point where one year later I can say that I am doing well and continuing my life. The dates and events below are based on my own journey and records.

Sometimes life changes in a single day. Mine changed on 13 September.

The timeline

What happened, step by step

From the first seizure to one year of treatment and recovery.

13 SEP 2025
01 · The First Seizure

The day everything changed

On 13 September, I had my first seizure and lost consciousness. I remained unconscious for approximately 9 hours. When I regained consciousness, I had already been referred between two hospitals.

14 SEP 2025
02 · First Diagnosis

CT → MRI → Low-grade glioma

A CT Head scan had already been performed and a brain tumor was detected. The following day, an MRI was performed and the tumor was described as a low-grade glioma. The MRI report available to me did not mention a tumor size.

SEP–OCT 2025
03 · Searching for Answers

Multiple consultations

I consulted Dr. Sunil Pradhan. Surgery was discussed as the required treatment. Gamma Ray treatment was also discussed, and I was advised to check at AIIMS. We then visited MAX Hospital, Lucknow, where repeat MRI and reassessment after some time were discussed.

12 OCT 2025
04 · AIIMS Delhi

Surgery enters the plan

On 12 October, I went to AIIMS Delhi. After consultation, surgery was recommended.

16 OCT 2025
05 · Second MRI

The decision becomes clearer

A second MRI was performed on 16 October. After reviewing it, the need for surgery was confirmed.

18 OCT 2025
06 · A Normal Moment in an Abnormal Time

Diwali

Even while facing a serious medical situation, I celebrated Diwali with my family on 18 October.

22 OCT 2025
07 · Apollo Lucknow

Preparing for surgery

On 22 October, I consulted neurosurgeon Dr. Chhitij Srivastava at Apollomedics Super Speciality Hospital, Lucknow. I was told that recovery after surgery could take around 10 days, and the hospital discussed the expected expenses.

During this period I was taking Levera 500 mg and sleep-related medication. I experienced irritability, anger and a lot of overthinking about the future — including plans to help people through social service after getting better.

17 NOV 2025
08 · The Critical Night

Severe headache and vomiting

On the evening of 17 November, my condition suddenly became serious. I developed a very severe headache and vomited two or three times. By morning, I was taken to the hospital and admitted.

The doctors scheduled surgery for the following day.

19 NOV 2025
09 · Surgery

19 November — Brain surgery

On 19 November, I underwent brain surgery.

23 NOV 2025
10 · Home

Back home after surgery

By 23 November, I was back at home. The next major step was waiting for the biopsy result.

LATE NOV 2025
11 · Biopsy

The diagnosis became more serious

The biopsy reported Grade 3/4 oligodendroglioma, with cancer cells identified. I was then referred to an oncologist for further treatment.

2025–2026
12 · Radiation + Chemotherapy

The next phase of treatment

After MRI-based planning, the radiation area and dose were determined. Alongside radiation, I was also taking an oral chemotherapy medicine, one tablet each morning around 9–10 AM.

My radiation treatment was delivered using RapidArc.

~3 MONTHS LATER
13 · Follow-up MRI

A small new lesion/tumor was detected

Around three months later, a follow-up MRI showed another small brain tumor/lesion. The chemotherapy tablet dose was subsequently increased by the doctor.

~6 MONTHS LATER
14 · The next MRI

The lesion had grown

Approximately three months after that MRI, another scan showed that the lesion had become approximately twice its previous size.

NEXT PHASE
15 · Tumor Board

A new treatment path

The two MRI reports were compared. A tumor board meeting was held, and a new treatment approach was chosen: targeted therapy. I then began receiving an injection as part of this treatment.

13 SEP 2026
16 · One Year Later

I made it through one year.

One year after the day everything started, I am doing well and living my life. Treatment and regular monitoring are still part of my routine.

Recovery

The hardest part was not only the treatment.

It was the uncertainty — waiting for reports, meeting doctors, hearing different possibilities, managing fear and still trying to imagine what life would look like after all of it.

A year later, the biggest difference is simple: I am here, I am functioning, and I am continuing forward.

13 September 2026

One year later, I am still moving forward.

The journey is not defined by one scan, one surgery or one medicine. It is a long process of treatment, monitoring, patience and living one day at a time.

— Ankit Singh
